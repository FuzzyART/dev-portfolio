# Hello, World

This is my portfolio, although that description is only part of the story.

This site is **not primarily a recruitment site**. Of course, I want it to show what I can do, and if a project here happens to convince someone that I would be a good fit for a job, that's great. But that isn't the main reason I built it.

I also want this site to be useful to me.

## A technical notebook

I have a bad habit of solving a problem once, forgetting how I solved it, and then having to solve it again six months later.

So some of the guides here are deliberately written for my future self.

How did I get this particular system running?
Why did I configure something that way?
Which dependencies were necessary?
What did I do when something inevitably broke?

I'd rather write it down once and have a place to find it later.

The same applies to infrastructure. If I decide to build some slightly unconventional CI/CD setup, I want to document **why** I chose it, not just what commands I eventually used. Sometimes the reasoning behind a questionable decision is more useful than the decision itself.

## Experiments without academic claims

I also want a place where I can experiment.

Machine learning is full of algorithms that have been described, analyzed, optimized, and published many times. That doesn't mean I can't take one of them apart and change something just to see what happens.

For example, I'm currently working with a Machine Learning algorithm. At some point I may decide to change part of the algorithm simply because I have an idea and want to find out what happens.

That isn't a new scientific contribution.

It doesn't need to be.

If an experiment produces an interesting result, I'll document it. If it produces garbage, I'll document that too. The point is to learn by actually building and testing things.

## Old projects get another chance

There are also quite a few projects in my personal archive that never really went anywhere.

Some were abandoned because the idea wasn't good enough. Some were abandoned because I ran out of time. Others were probably abandoned for reasons I no longer remember.

I'd like to resurrect some of them.

They may turn out to be useful. They may turn out to be terrible. Either way, bringing them back to life gives me an opportunity to look at old code and old ideas with a different perspective.

Some of those projects may become proper portfolio pieces. Others may remain experiments.

That's fine.

## So what is this site?

Part portfolio, part technical notebook, part laboratory, and part archaeological excavation.

I'll use it to document projects as they evolve, write guides for things I don't want to forget, experiment with algorithms and software, and occasionally bring an old idea back from the dead.

If someone finds something useful here along the way, even better.

For now, this is the beginning.
